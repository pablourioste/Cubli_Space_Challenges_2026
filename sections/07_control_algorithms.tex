% ================= 7. CONTROL ALGORITHMS & APPROACH =================
\section{Control Algorithms \& Approach}
\label{sec:control_algorithms}

\subsection{What has to be regulated}
\label{sec:problem-statement}

Balanced on a corner, the cube is an inverted pendulum in every direction
perpendicular to the vertical. Linearised about the balancing attitude it has
two unstable eigenvalues,
%
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_{1,2} = 8.2572,\; 8.2343\ \mathrm{s^{-1}},
  \qquad
  \lambda = \sqrt{\frac{S_g}{\bar{\bm{\Theta}}}},
  \label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
%
a divergence time constant of 121\,ms. Nothing in the open-loop plant resists
displacement: the gravitational moment grows with tilt rather than opposing
it. The controller supplies all of the stability there is.

Three properties of the plant set the shape of the control problem, and each
is addressed by a specific part of what follows.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The attitude state is an element of $SO(3)$, not a vector.}
        No three-parameter global parameterisation of $SO(3)$ exists without
        a singularity, and the operating point sits $53.94^\circ$ from the
        orientation in which the geometry was authored, so small-angle
        reasoning about the parameterisation itself is not available
        (\S\ref{sec:attitude-representation}).
  \item \textbf{One rotational degree of freedom is degenerate at both ends.}
        Gravity produces no moment about the vertical, and a single vector
        measurement cannot observe rotation about it. The problem is
        eight-dimensional, not nine (\S\ref{sec:structural}).
  \item \textbf{Authority is bounded by stored momentum, not by torque.}
        The wheels exchange momentum with the body rather than pushing
        against the world, so recovery consumes wheel momentum and the
        envelope is set by what the wheels can store before saturating
        (\S\ref{sec:nonlinearities}).
\end{enumerate}

\label{sec:architecture}

The complete per-cycle cost is three cross products, one $3\times9$
matrix--vector product, one reciprocal square root and three \texttt{tanhf}
calls --- roughly 60 multiply--accumulates, with no \texttt{atan2} and no

consumed by CAN traffic, not by the control computation.

\subsection{Attitude representation}
\label{sec:attitude-representation}

This is the load-bearing decision of the control design. Three
representations were implemented and instrumented before the loop closed; two
failed in ways that resembled tuning problems and were not.

\subsubsection{Euler angles: three measured failures}
\label{sec:euler-failures}

The first implementation used a $Z$--$Y$--$X$ Euler triple. It failed three
times, in three distinct ways.

\paragraph{Failure 1 --- the parameterisation went singular, after passing
four gates.} The Simscape plant was built with a Gimbal joint on defensible
grounds: it linearises to a clean $9\times9$ system comparable directly
against the hand-derived $\bm{A}$ and $\bm{B}$, the equilibrium gimbal angles
are $[0,0,0]$ because the mount transform pre-applies the corner tilt, and
lock sits roughly $75^\circ$ away at corner-balance amplitudes. It passed
validation gates~1 to~4 on that basis, including the linearisation gate at
$\lVert\Delta\bm{A}\rVert = 1.742\times10^{-7}$,
$\lVert\Delta\bm{B}\rVert = 2.157\times10^{-16}$ (measured on the
$(+1,+1,+1)$ configuration; see \S\ref{sec:validation}).

It failed later, in closed-loop simulation, with a diagnostic signature:
$q_x$ and $q_z$ smooth throughout, while $q_y$ --- the middle gimbal
primitive, the one carrying the singularity --- spiked to $\pm20000^\circ$
and returned within milliseconds. A rigid body cannot rotate that far in that
interval, and the confinement of the excursion to the middle primitive
identifies the cause exactly: the Jacobian relating gimbal rates to body
rates diverging, with the solver integrating the divergence.

It also retrospectively explained a persistent $19.8\,\%$ error in the
gravity block of $\bm{A}$ that never resolved while $\bm{B}$ stayed exact at
$3\times10^{-16}$. That asymmetry is the tell: $\bm{B}$ does not involve the
attitude parameterisation and $\bm{A}$ does, so a discrepancy appearing in
one and not the other is a coordinates error rather than a mass-properties
or wiring error.

The remedy is a Spherical joint, which carries no chart at the cost of a
rank-deficient linearisation.

\paragraph{Failure 2 --- an Euler triple is not the reduced attitude.} Even
far from lock, the triple $[q_1,q_2,q_3]$ is not a rotation vector, so
projecting out the vertical component removes yaw only to first order.

On this cube yaw is not small, for a mechanical reason. The inertia about the
vertical is
%
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\bm{g}}_B^{\top}\bm{\Theta}\,\hat{\bm{g}}_B
  = 5.374\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2},
\end{equation}
%
against a mean tilt inertia of $2.825\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ in the
plane perpendicular to $\hat{\bm{g}}_B$ --- the cube is $5.3\times$ lighter in
yaw than in tilt. Wheel momentum along $\hat{\bm{g}}_B$ therefore spins the
body at $I_s/(\hat{\bm{g}}_B^{\top}\bm{\Theta}\hat{\bm{g}}_B)
= 0.0906\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$ per $\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$ of
$\bm{\rho}\cdot\hat{\bm{g}}_B$. Released from $3.6^\circ$, yaw reaches
$23.6^\circ$ within $0.3\,$s --- entirely physical and
momentum-conserving, and far outside the range where a first-order yaw
removal is valid. Feeding the Euler triple to the controller cost
$19.5\,\%$ of the recovery angle, and the loop diverged.

\paragraph{Failure 3 --- Euler rates are not body rates.} Found only after
the first two were fixed. The Euler-rate vector
$[\dot q_1,\dot q_2,\dot q_3]$ equals the body angular velocity $\bm{\omega}$
only at $\bm{q}=\bm{0}$; over the recovery trajectory the discrepancy reached
$73\,\%$. Correcting both the attitude and the rate feedback moved the
Simscape recovery from $3.33^\circ$ to $4.14^\circ$, at which point it
matched an independently written nonlinear simulator to $0.35\,\%$.

\medskip
\noindent
All three are the same mistake in different clothes: a signal correct at the
equilibrium was treated as correct everywhere. That is precisely the
assumption a balancing controller is not entitled to make, because the
controller only acts when the system is away from equilibrium.

\subsubsection{Reduced attitude}
\label{sec:reduced-attitude}

Let $\hat{\bm{\gamma}}$ be the unit vector along gravity expressed in the
cube body frame, and $\hat{\bm{g}}_B$ its value at the balanced attitude --- a
per-corner constant. Then
%
\begin{align}
  \hat{\bm{\gamma}} &= \mathrm{normalise}(-\bm{a}_{\mathrm{cal}})
    && \text{directly what the accelerometer gives}
    \label{eq:gam-meas}\\[0.2em]
  \dot{\hat{\bm{\gamma}}} &= -\,\bm{\omega}\times\hat{\bm{\gamma}}
    && \text{kinematics, one line, no chart}
    \label{eq:gam-kin}\\[0.2em]
  \bm{\phi} &= -\,\hat{\bm{g}}_B\times\hat{\bm{\gamma}}
    && \text{the entire attitude computation}
    \label{eq:phi}
\end{align}
%
Three numbers, one norm constraint, two degrees of freedom;
$\hat{\bm{\gamma}}=\hat{\bm{g}}_B$ is balanced.

The property that does the work is that $\bm{\phi}$ in \eqref{eq:phi} is
perpendicular to $\hat{\bm{g}}_B$ \emph{by construction} --- a cross product
with $\hat{\bm{g}}_B$ always is. Yaw therefore cannot enter the tilt channel.
Not approximately, not to first order: exactly, at any attitude, at any tilt
magnitude. This is the direct repair of Failure~2, where the projection had
to be applied as a correction valid only near equilibrium.

Each property was checked against the nonlinear model rather than assumed:

\subsubsection{The limits of this choice}

Stating them is part of the argument. The correction term
$\bm{e}=\hat{\bm{g}}\times\hat{\bm{\gamma}}$ has magnitude
$\sin(\text{error})$: well conditioned below $90^\circ$, but zero again at
$180^\circ$, so an inverted cube can lock onto the antipode. The mitigation
is a reset when $\hat{\bm{g}}\cdot\hat{\bm{\gamma}}<0$ for $N$ consecutive
cycles. Reduced attitude is also the right choice \emph{because} yaw is
irrelevant to corner balancing; jump-up and corner-to-corner locomotion,
where $\hat{\bm{g}}_B$ moves through $67$--$107^\circ$, require large-attitude
tracking against a full reference and a quaternion MEKF becomes the
appropriate structure. Finally, a magnetometer would not help: it would make
yaw observable while leaving it uncontrollable, and the degeneracy is
two-sided (\S\ref{sec:structural}).
