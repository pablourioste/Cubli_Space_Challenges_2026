\subsection{Work Breakdown Structure}
\label{sec:wbs}

This plan runs from project start to the final presentation on 20~August 2026.
The work is grouped into five streams --- investigation, requirements and
documentation; control and software; hardware design and build; three-dimensional
design; and electrical and electronics --- led by the owners assigned in
Table~\ref{tab:team_roles}. The investigation is kept to the essentials needed to
substantiate the design: a focused literature review, the dynamics derivation,
and the wheel sizing that follows from it. Table~\ref{tab:wbs_tasks} breaks the
streams into individual tasks, each with its scope, single accountable lead and
dates.

\subsection{Schedule and Contingency Plan}
\label{sec:schedule}

The full-page schedule in Figure~\ref{fig:gantt} places every task of
Section~\ref{sec:wbs} on the 23~July--20~August 2026 calendar, and
Table~\ref{tab:gates} states the gate that closes each phase together with its
status as at the date of issue. The plan is stated first, as it was baselined;
what actually happened against it is recorded in the notes and the
\emph{Schedule as run} paragraph that follow the table.

\paragraph{Structure of the plan.}
The first two weeks establish the analytical and design foundation: the dynamics
derivation and wheel sizing on the documentation side, the single-axis rig and
its wiring design on the hardware and electrical sides, and the simulation
environment on the control side. These converge on 2~August, which carries two
objectives: the first powered bring-up of the single-axis rig, taking the
\qty{5}{\volt} rail, the driver and the encoder to verified open-loop operation
(M1); and the freeze of the three-dimensional frame geometry (M2a), against
which the mass, inertia and CoM budget is then closed (M2b). The frame freeze
releases the second procurement round on 5~August (M3), which is the last point
at which parts can be ordered with useful delivery margin before cube assembly
begins on 13~August.

The documentation stream carries two assessed submissions. The first is delivered
on 3~August (G4) with the evidence available at that date; the assessor's
comments are triaged during the following week and the corrected second draft is
submitted on 14~August (M8), by which point the rig results and the integrated
cube can both be reported.

\paragraph{Critical path and contingency.}
The critical path runs from the 2~August bring-up through the closed-loop rig
(M4), the measured plant parameters (M6) and the single-axis control design
(M7), into the three-dimensional controller and the balancing demonstrations.
The single-axis rig is the protected item, since its balance result is the
experimental evidence in the document. The three-dimensional design proceeds in
parallel on the mechanical side, so that fabrication is not held by the control
work.

The plan carries the full scope through to the jump-up demonstration. The final
week is nonetheless tightly constrained: edge balance begins two days after cube
assembly completes, and the jump-up gate falls on the day of the final
presentation. The jump-up (S3) is therefore designated sacrificial scope by
standing decision. If the balancing demonstrations require the time, the jump-up
yields first, and the demonstration presented on 20~August is the corner balance
and commanded slew. The brake mechanism build and its associated electrical work
are sequenced after the edge-balance gate for the same reason.

Appendix~\ref{app:risks} lists the risks behind this plan, with their
mitigation and owner, and fixes the order scope is cut if the schedule runs
short.

\paragraph{Notes on the gates not closed.} Five entries above are not a clean
pass, and each is recorded here rather than absorbed into the status column.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{M2b --- budget closure.} The v1 gate M2 bundled two separable
things: freezing the frame geometry, and closing the mass, inertia and CoM
budget against the sizing analysis. The first was done on the planned date and
is what \qty{\cubeL}{\milli\metre} rests on; the second was not, and stating
both as one passed gate overstated the design's position. They are therefore
split. M2b remains open because the structure line of
Table~\ref{tab:massbudget} is still an allowance rather than a CAD roll-up
(\qty{\massStructAllow}{\gram} projected against \qty{\massStructBuilt}{\gram}
implied by the weighed article), and because the CoM offset has not been
measured. What \emph{is} closed is the
mass cap: the integrated cube weighs \qty{\Mbuilt}{\kilo\gram} against the
\qty{\Mcap}{\kilo\gram} of CON-02. M2b closes when the CAD mass properties are
exported and the CoM offset measured, not before.

\item \textbf{M2a --- geometry freeze.} Closed as a design action: the CAD is
frozen at \qty{\cubeL}{\milli\metre} and every driving dimension of
Table~\ref{tab:cad_params} is referred to it. The CON-01 \emph{inspection} ---
measuring the built frame against that figure --- is still open in
Appendix~\ref{app:verification}, so the gate is reported as frozen in CAD and
unverified on the article. The related \qty{\cubeLan}{\milli\metre} versus
\qty{\cubeL}{\milli\metre} discrepancy between the sizing run and the frozen
value is a separate open decision, recorded in
Section~\ref{sec:requirements} and in the footnote to
Table~\ref{tab:spec_dims}.

\item \textbf{M4 --- closed loop and safety path.} The closed loop is live and
its result is Table~\ref{tab:balance_examples}. The second half of the
criterion is not evidenced: FUN-08 (disarm from every mode) and FUN-09 (fall
response) are both Open in Appendix~\ref{app:verification}. The gate is
therefore partly met, and the safety-path demonstration is outstanding.

\item \textbf{M5 --- manufacturing package.} Released in fact: the wheels,
frame and rig parts of Table~\ref{tab:cad_parts} were fabricated from it and
the cube is integrated.

\item \textbf{M6 --- plant parameters.} Open. The parameters of
Table~\ref{tab:params} --- $I_b$, $l$, $l_b$, $C_b$, $C_w$ --- have
determination methods assigned by Section~\ref{sec:paramid} but no measured
values, and are recorded as outstanding in Appendix~\ref{app:specs} rather than
estimated. The 1-DoF controller was therefore designed and tuned without a
measured plant; that it balances (G3) does not close M6, and the reconciliation
against the sizing analysis that M6 asks for has not been performed.

\item \textbf{G6 --- edge balance.} Edge balance has been achieved on the cube
with the 1-DoF control law (Section~\ref{sec:test_3dof}), but the
\qty{60}{\second} duration and the disturbance-recovery half of the criterion
are not yet measured: PER-01 is Partial (rig verified, cube open) and PER-04 is
Open. Recorded as partly met.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Schedule as run.} This document is issued at the end of the window
it plans, so Table~\ref{tab:gates} is a record and not a forecast. The plan held
its shape: the critical path ran as laid out and the protected item, the 1-DoF
rig, delivered its balance result on time (G3, 12~Aug). Two things moved. The
second assessed submission slipped from 14~to \docdate{} --- the corrections
were larger than the one-week triage window assumed --- which compressed the
final week further, exactly the risk Section~\ref{sec:schedule} flags above.
And the analysis-side gates M2b and M6 were carried rather than closed: both
are measurement tasks that were displaced by build and control work, which is
the honest reason and is now visible in the register of
Appendix~\ref{app:risks}. The sacrificial-scope decision was taken as written:
G7 and G8 stand open at issue, and the demonstration presented on 20~August is
the one the standing decision protects.
