\section{System Requirements and Verification}
\label{sec:requirements}

Section~\ref{sec:objectives} states what this project must achieve. This
section states what the \emph{system} must do for those objectives to be met,
and how each statement will be shown to have been met.

The distinction is one of abstraction and verifiability, not of subject matter.
An objective is an outcome --- for the machine or for the student --- and is
not directly testable: ``the system must maintain multi-axis closed-loop
balance about a point-contact equilibrium'' names a capability but no threshold
and no method. A requirement is a single \emph{shall} statement about the
system, carrying a number or a binary condition, a parent objective, and a
verification method, so that it can be shown to pass or fail. The rewriting of
the objective above into requirements FUN-02 and PER-03 is what makes it
something the design can be held to.

Two conventions are worth stating because they are easy to violate. First, a
requirement says \emph{what}, never \emph{how}: the choice of an LQR law, a
complementary filter, a ballasted PET-CF ring or a particular driver is a
design decision belonging to Sections~\ref{sec:sizing}--\ref{sec:control_algorithms},
and writing it into a requirement would remove the freedom those sections spend
their length exercising. Second, some requirements are \emph{derived} rather
than imposed --- marked~(D) below --- because they are outputs of an analysis
rather than inputs to it. PER-07 is the clearest case: the wheel-inertia figure
is not a free choice but a consequence of the cube mass and edge length, which
are themselves outputs of the closure procedure of
Section~\ref{sec:massbudget}. Recording it as derived keeps that dependency
visible instead of presenting a computed result as an external demand.

\paragraph{Verification methods.} Each requirement carries one or more of:
\textbf{A}nalysis, closure by calculation or simulation against a stated model;
\textbf{I}nspection, confirmation against drawings, a parts list or a
measurement of the built article; \textbf{T}est, instrumented measurement
against a numerical criterion; and \textbf{D}emonstration, observed execution of
a behaviour without instrumented measurement. The procedure and acceptance
criterion behind each entry are given in Appendix~\ref{app:verification}, which
is also where evidence is recorded as testing proceeds.

\paragraph{Scope classes.} Requirements are marked \textbf{M} (mandatory, a
committed deliverable), \textbf{S} (stretch, scoped but sacrificial --- the
jump-up is designated sacrificial scope by standing decision,
Section~\ref{sec:schedule}), or \textbf{U} (unscheduled, retained so the scope
boundary is explicit; no gate exists for it in
Table~\ref{tab:gates}).

\paragraph{On the educational objectives.} The learning objectives of
Table~\ref{tab:edu_traceability} are deliberately \emph{not} decomposed into
requirements here. They are constraints on how the engineering is done and
documented rather than properties of the delivered hardware, and the provisions
that satisfy them are stated where they take effect --- the parametric CAD of
Section~\ref{sec:cad}, the adjustable ballast of Section~\ref{sec:rw_sizing},
the staged validation of Section~\ref{sec:methodology}, the bring-up protocol
of Section~\ref{sec:electrical_safety} and the bill of materials of
Appendix~\ref{app:bom}. This section is therefore technical throughout.

\paragraph{Status at the time of writing.} PER-01 and PER-04 are verified on
the single-axis rig (Section~\ref{sec:test_1dof}); PER-04 is demonstrated to
\qty{24}{\degree}, which is the rig's mechanical hard stop rather than a limit
of the controller, so the requirement is stated at the largest angle for which
a recovery time was recorded. PER-07 and PER-10 are closed by analysis and by
rig measurement respectively, pending confirmation on the integrated cube.
Every remaining requirement is open. Appendix~\ref{app:verification} carries
the per-requirement status and is the record updated as testing proceeds.

\paragraph{Relationship to the milestone gates.} The gates of
Table~\ref{tab:gates} are scheduling events; the requirements above are
properties of the system. They meet where a gate's pass criterion is a
requirement: G3 closes on PER-01, G6 on PER-02 and PER-05, G7 on FUN-02,
FUN-03 and PER-03, and G8 on FUN-10 and PER-11. A gate can slip without a
requirement changing, and a requirement can fail at a gate that the schedule
nonetheless passes; keeping the two separate is what allows the jump-up to be
dropped as sacrificial scope without any other requirement being renegotiated.

\paragraph{Open items.} Three requirements rest on figures not yet fixed.
PER-08 and PER-09 depend on the achievable wheel speed at end-of-discharge
voltage, currently derived rather than measured; the spin-up test of WBS
task~E10 closes this. PER-11 has no measurement and is scoped with the jump-up.
CON-01 is stated at \qty{150}{\milli\metre} to match the frame freeze of gate
M2, while the sizing analysis of Section~\ref{sec:sizing} is currently run at
\qty{149}{\milli\metre}; the discrepancy is small but not negligible at the
present inertia margin, and is to be reconciled at CAD closure.
