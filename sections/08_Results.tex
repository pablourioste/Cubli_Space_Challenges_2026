\section{Results}
\subsection{Validation Results to Date}
\label{sec:validation_results}

This subsection reports evidence against the acceptance criteria of
Table~\ref{tab:requirements}, in the order the stages are entered. Where no
result exists yet, the criterion is stated so the gap is explicit rather than
silent. Appendix~\ref{app:verification} carries the per-requirement
verification status that the results below close out.

\subsubsection{1-DoF Control Validation}
\label{sec:test_1dof}

The single-axis rig is the protected experimental item of
Section~\ref{sec:schedule}, and its balance result is the evidence carried into
this document. Gate G3 requires the rig to balance for at least 60~s unassisted.

Beyond the duration criterion, three quantities are recorded because they
determine whether the result transfers to the integrated cube rather than merely
holding on the rig. Recovery from a defined impulse establishes that the balance
is a stabilised equilibrium and not a fortunate initial condition. Steady-state
wheel-speed drift measures momentum accumulation: a controller that balances
while monotonically spinning up is one that will saturate, and the drift rate
predicts when. Control effort is compared against the continuous and peak current
limits of the driver given in Appendix~\ref{app:bom}, since a balance sustained
only by exceeding the continuous rating is not a balance that survives to the
demonstration.

upright equilibrium with no applied disturbance.

the physical hard stop of the experimental rig support as well as the maximum tilt
evaluated in simulation, so requirement PER-03 (recovery from
\qty{12}{\degree} or more) is satisfied with margin, and the demonstrated
envelope is bounded by the rig rather than by the controller.

\subsubsection{3-DoF Control Validation}
\label{sec:test_3dof}

Both cube equilibria have now been exercised on hardware: corner balance,
under quiet hold and under repeated pushes to failure, and edge balance,
under quiet hold and recovery from a released tilt. Two firmware
configurations were run on the corner: the autotrim baseline
(\texttt{Stage4\_AutoTrim.ino}) and the same law with a body-rate low-pass
filter added (\texttt{Stage4\_AutoTrim\_RateFilter.ino}, discussed further
against the vibration source it targets in \S\ref{sec:vibration}).
Table~\ref{tab:balance_3dof_template} updates the result template with these
runs against the Gate~G3 threshold of \qty{60}{\second} underlying PER-01 and
PER-02, and Table~\ref{tab:recovery_angle_3dof_template} carries the edge
release test mirroring Table~\ref{tab:recovery_angle} against PER-04; the
corner disturbance-rejection data uses a different protocol (repeated live
pushes of unmeasured magnitude, not a single release from a set angle) and is
reported separately in Table~\ref{tab:corner_pushes}. Driver current was not
logged in these runs -- effort is reported as the commanded torque
$|\tau_{\text{cmd}}|$ actually measured, not converted from it.

\paragraph{Corner quiet hold.} Figure~\ref{fig:corner_quiet_phi} compares
tilt magnitude between the two configurations over their respective armed
windows. Read at face value it favours autotrim (RMS \qty{0.39}{\degree}
vs \qty{0.46}{\degree}), but the comparison is confounded on two axes and
should not be read as a verdict on the filter: the rate-filter session's
own \emph{raw}, pre-filter gyro RMS is more than double the autotrim
session's (\qty{18.6}{\degree\per\second} vs \qty{8.8}{\degree\per\second}),
which is a difference in bench conditions before either firmware touches
the signal, not a filter effect; and the rate-filter build also ships a
faster trim-adaptation gain ($k_a$: \num{2.922e-6} to \num{1e-4}, a
\qty{10}{\second} time constant against the baseline's \qty{60}{\second}),
so the wheel-speed roll-over visible only in the rate-filter trace of
Figure~\ref{fig:corner_quiet_wheel_effort} is at least as plausibly $k_a$
converging faster as it is the filter removing noise from the control law.
Both changes ship in the same build; this data cannot separate them.
Since $f$ (filter corner) and $k_a$ are both live-settable on the same
firmware, the controlled version of this test -- one bench session,
toggling each independently -- is the next run, not a repeat of the
two-session comparison.

\paragraph{Rate-filter frequency response.} One comparison within this data
\emph{is} clean: raw versus filtered body rate within the rate-filter
session alone, same samples, only the filter differing.
Figure~\ref{fig:corner_quiet_om_filter_effect} shows overall
$|\omega|$ RMS dropping from \qty{18.6}{\degree\per\second} raw to
\qty{10.3}{\degree\per\second} filtered, a real \qty{45}{\percent}
reduction. The PSD in Figure~\ref{fig:corner_quiet_psd} shows why that
headline number is not the whole story: there is a sharp structural mode
at \qtyrange{29.7}{30.5}{\hertz} on all three axes -- the same mode
\S\ref{sec:vibration} places inside the control passband rather than above
the $200$\,Hz previously assumed -- and the \qty{20}{\hertz} first-order
filter attenuates it by only $\approx$6~dB (a factor of $\approx 2$), not
the 20--30~dB claimed in the header comments of
\texttt{Stage4\_AutoTrim\_RateFilter.ino} and
\texttt{Stage5\_AutoTrim\_RateFilter.ino}, a documentation error flagged
for correction there. The filter is doing something real, just not what
the comment claims (\S\ref{sec:vibration} gives the full frequency-response
argument).

\paragraph{Corner disturbance rejection.} Table~\ref{tab:corner_pushes} and
Figure~\ref{fig:corner_pushes_timeline} report repeated pushes applied by
hand until the controller trips; no load cell measured push force
directly, so severity is read from the peak tilt reached, all below the
\qty{5}{\degree} PER-04 threshold. Both configurations recover pushes of
essentially the same size and fail at essentially the same severity --
\qty{24.0}{\degree} (autotrim) and \qty{23.8}{\degree} (rate filter)
immediately before hitting the same \qty{25}{\degree} tilt trip
(\texttt{kMaxTilt}) that disarms the drivers in both builds, which is a
property of that shared firmware threshold, not of the rate filter.
Recovery time to under \qty{1}{\degree} is statistically indistinguishable
between configurations (\qtyrange{0.08}{0.20}{\second} across all recovered
pushes). This test does not show either configuration tolerating a larger
disturbance than the other, and PER-04 (recovery from \qty{5}{\degree} or
more) has not yet been exercised on the cube by either configuration.

\paragraph{Edge quiet hold.} Figure~\ref{fig:edge_quiet_phi} shows
\qty{196.96}{\second} of armed edge balance, RMS \qty{0.58}{\degree} --
clearing the PER-01 threshold with over three times the required duration.
That RMS is not, however, representative of an undisturbed hold throughout:
a $\approx\qty{3.5}{\degree}$ excursion between roughly
$t=\qty{100}{\second}$ and $t=\qty{135}{\second}$ self-recovers with no
armed dropout and no logged marker to explain it. Outside that window the
trace is consistent with plain sensor/torque noise near zero, similar in
character to the corner quiet-hold runs above. The excursion should be
repeated and identified before \qty{0.58}{\degree} is quoted elsewhere as
the undisturbed edge noise floor.

\paragraph{Edge recovery from a released tilt.} Released from
\qty{-6.18}{\degree}, the edge equilibrium does not recover cleanly; it
rings down (Figure~\ref{fig:edge_recovery_6deg}). Tilt overshoots to
\qty{+5.05}{\degree} at $t=\qty{0.70}{\second}$ -- within \qty{1}{\degree}
of the release magnitude, on the opposite side -- before damping wins.
Table~\ref{tab:edge_ringdown} gives the full sequence of threshold
crossings: a naive "first time under \qty{1}{\degree}"
($t=\qty{1.89}{\second}$) reads as a fast recovery and is misleading, since
the trace keeps ringing (visible bumps back to $\approx\qty{0.9}{\degree}$
as late as $t=\qty{8}{\second}$) for several seconds afterward. An
overshoot this close to 1:1 from a 6\textdegree{} release suggests the
closed-loop edge poles are lightly damped rather than merely slow, and is
worth checking directly against the edge control law's pole placement
(\S\ref{sec:lqr}).
